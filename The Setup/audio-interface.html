Audio Interface:

Rode Streamer X | $249.99

So far, the Rode Streamer X accomplishes what it needs to do.

It functions as an amp for the Shure SM7B microphone, providing power and a gain knob.

It also features a headphone volume knob, which is really helpful for adjusting the volume.

One of the main deciding factors in purchasing this device was the HDMI capture card, which allows me to connect my Sony ZV-E10 mirrorless camera.

A lot of my teammates get a kick out of the voice effects, such as the chipmunk and robot voices.

Although it does what I need it to do, I am not a huge fan of the companion software—it has been pretty glitchy, though updates have helped a little. For the price I wish it felt more polished: you are paying a lot for the hardware, but the software still matters day to day.

Rode Central still does not save your settings after restarting your PC unless you replace the file generated by the program, and sometimes the HDMI capture card struggles to pick up the signal from my mirrorless camera.

Mic routing, mobile mics, and NVIDIA Broadcast

One limitation that bit me: I cannot get the mic output behavior I want straight from the unit alone, so I route through NVIDIA Broadcast instead. When the AC is loud and I am on a phone or other “mobile style” mic, Broadcast’s noise reduction is pretty good—not magic, but it helps a lot.

Because Broadcast uses the GPU, games can hitch a little when it is working hard. I would also skip the “Studio Voice” style mode: on my setup it made stutters worse and sounded overly processed, almost like an AI “studio” preset rather than a natural voice.
